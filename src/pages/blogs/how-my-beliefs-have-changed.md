I couldn't sing. I just lost the joy.

I was grateful for the community I had and the friends I had. But something about what I was doing
felt very worn out, like it was at the end of what it could do for me.

### Where It Began

My story is a common one, in a lot of ways.

I grew up with a wonderful Christian community around me. Lots of kids, lots of people, and we
spent most of our time together. We were insular, and we had real unity: unity in what we believed
and unity with each other. Those were some really great years.

I felt like I had the answers for everything. Everything I needed for life could be found in friends
and family in the church. In my particular church.

I was not good at being regular in prayer. But I was an avid reader, and I loved reading the Bible.
I believed that in studying it I would find salvation. Enlightenment. Freedom to live a life free
from sin, or at least a life less encumbered by my own problems. In my own way, I was a follower of
Jesus in that time.

### The Crack

As I grew up, that started to crack. The idea that my church could solve all my problems began to
crumble. I was losing the joy I once found in the routines of Christian living.

I needed help, and I felt I had to reach outside my Christian bubble to get it. So I did.

That is what started everything. Reaching outside led me to question the thing sitting underneath
all of it: that my church, my family, and my way of practicing Christianity had all the answers.
Once that question was open, I started exploring.

### It Came Apart

When I went down that road, my body began reacting to the way I was practicing my devotions. I could
not do them the way I always had, and I could not find a new way either.

I thought this meant I was being an undisciplined Christian.

This was part of what people call deconstruction, and it just sort of happened. My mind wanted to
follow Christ. My body was telling me no. I was exhausted, and in the end I had to let it go.

I could not explain it yet. But my body had a sense of something before my mind could grasp it, and
my mind caught up much later.

Prayer was okay. I could still pray, though I did not do it often. And when I did, it was stripped of
all ceremony I once knew. And I thought quite often about the scriptures I had read as a boy.

It was during this time that I clung to the words of Christ. Jesus said, "you will know the truth,
and the truth will set you free."

I did not know where I was going. But I set myself a test. I would know I had found the right thing
when I started experiencing freedom, joy, excitement, and optimism, instead of the dread I had come
to associate with my very worn out Christian practices.

That test is the reason for everything that follows.

### The Lens I Could Not See

Part of what I was working through was that I had a lens.

I was reading scripture through something I had inherited from my childhood and my home church. What
they gave me was a wonderful start, and I'm thankful for it. But I was starting to wonder whether it
was the full picture.

I could not have described the lens at the time. I only knew that I had one, and that I had to find
out what it was.

I was not only looking for another way of reading scripture. I wanted one that would tell me the
truth, and that would give me the key I needed to practice the kind of Christianity I wanted. There
was just something insufficient, I felt, about the lens I had.

So I started looking for help, and I had no idea where to go. This was really before AI could help
you search these things out.

### A Bold Email

My brother-in-law Joey has a podcast, and he brings in a lot of different voices. One of his guests
was an author whose book had really impacted me, and I listened to the whole episode.

Because Joey had him on the show, Joey had his private email. So I asked for it, and I sent a bold
request for a video call. I got one.

The call was awkward, and I was flustered. But he told me two things I needed to hear. He said the
hard part is coming back, and that I would make it. And he told me my journey was not uncommon. It
was far more common than I thought.

Then he gave me a reading list.

### Losing My Categories

The books took things away from me before they gave me anything back.

The first thing I lost was heaven. I had always been taught that we die and we go to heaven or
hell. Resurrection was just a nicer word for going to heaven. The kingdom of God was just a nicer
word for going to heaven.

N.T. Wright showed me that the Bible tells a different story. Heaven and earth were joined in the
beginning, when God walked in the garden and the world was flourishing. And the vision the prophets
kept describing was not about escaping earth. It was about heaven and earth being rejoined.

What Wright gave me was not more certainty about individual verses. It was the longest story, from
Genesis to Revelation. I have come to believe that the longest story that can be told will bring the
most cohesiveness.

In *Surprised by Hope*, I learned that the resurrection means God's future is already breaking into
the present through the body of Christ. Paul says the same thing in 1 Corinthians 15. Christ is the
firstfruits, and our body will be like his body. A body for this world.

Jesus never challenged the resurrection belief of his day. He confirmed it. Resurrection was bodily,
in this world, into a renewed creation.

### The Failure Was Not What I Thought

Somewhere in there I read John H. Walton's *The Lost World of Genesis One*, and it changed how I read
the first chapter of the Bible.

Genesis 1 is not a report on the physical creation of the world. The first act is God taking a
watery mess of chaos and pulling order out of it.

Walton's point is that Genesis tells us the why of things. What end is all this going toward, and
what is our place in it. And the answer is that we are images of God, placed here like idols in a
temple, so that the world and other people can experience the presence of God through us.

Which means our failure is a failure of vocation. God gave us a job, to rule this world so it would
flourish, and we failed at it. Paul says creation is in anguish, waiting for the sons of God to be
revealed.

### God Takes On the Cost

Reading Genesis this way changed the shape of the whole story for me.

Adam and Eve do what seems right in their own eyes. Suddenly they are naked. They realize they are
vulnerable and they need protection.

What God does next is small and easy to skip. He takes an animal out of his own garden, kills it,
and covers them. You are covered. You are equipped for this new reality. I am going to take on the
cost of making you whole again.

That is the pattern for the rest of scripture. God takes on the cost. He does not throw this good
creation away. He redeems it. He restores it.

### Where the Hell Is Hell

If God is redeeming this world rather than discarding it, then everything else has to move too. I
started wondering where the hell is hell.

I had thought hell was a place you go. If the destiny of heaven and earth is to be rejoined, is hell
going to be on earth? I have turned this over many times, from many angles, and I still struggle
with it today.

Honestly, I just do not see a story in the Bible that gives us a lot of clarity here.

I do believe in final judgment. I believe in the separation of the sheep and the goats. I believe
things will be made right. I know there is a lake of fire in scripture, and it seems pretty final.

I also read David Bentley Hart's *That All Shall Be Saved*, which makes a philosophical argument. If
God is the beginning and the end, and only good remains when the story of the universe is finished,
then evil has to be temporary. It cannot still be there when the curtains close, because that would
make God its creator.

I came to believe that when God is done, there will be no evil left. Which means he will deal with
it. Hart made me question whether hell ultimately exists at all. I do not have an answer for that.

What I do see at the end is a city garden. It tells me that God always purposed that we would be
joined with him, and that heaven and earth would be rejoined. It is a city as well as a garden,
which tells me that our own contributions somehow get caught up into this new world.

### The Gospel Was Bigger Than I Knew

Then I read Scot McKnight's *The King Jesus Gospel*, and it named the thing I had been circling for
years.

What I had learned so well was the way of salvation. What I had not learned was the gospel.

The account I was given was penal substitutionary atonement: Jesus took the punishment I deserved.
I am still grappling with that one, honestly. What I had not seen was that it is one meaning among
many, and I had been holding it as though it were all the cross was good for.

But here is what McKnight made me see. There are four books in the Bible that describe themselves as
the gospel. Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John. And I hardly ever took any theology from them. I read the
gospels for stories I could apply to my own life. I read the Old Testament mostly to find the
prophecies that pointed to Christ.

Mark opens by calling his book "the beginning of the gospel." Then he tells stories. Across the four
gospels the shape is a sequence: the heralding of the Christ, his birth, his appearance in Nazareth,
his anointing as Messiah, his passion, his death, his resurrection, and his ascension. Then, in the
preaching that followed, the giving of the Spirit.

The creeds are distillations of that. The long stories were carried around and poured through them.
Paul himself passed one along in 1 Corinthians 15.

So the gospel is not a sentence. It is a set of stories that have power in them when they are told.
That makes sense to me.

### What This Did to My Parenting

I had felt something like this instinctively, well before I had any reason for it.

I did not want to indoctrinate my children. I wanted to tell them the stories. It was only later,
while I was working through Wright, that I understood why. It is the telling of the story that
matters. It is the largest story, and the story carries far more than any doctrine I could hand
down.

I want my children to know what I think is the truth. But I have struggled with the idea of teaching
them specific doctrines. I am much more comfortable in this faith, because now I can just tell them
the stories and leave the rest to them.

I will consider my parenting a success if all I do is equip them with the stories, for them to
ponder and carry with them on their own journey.

### The Cross Was Never the Climax

Here is the part I did not expect.

If you read the gospels as complete stories rather than as setup for the epistles, they do not
climax at the cross. Mark climaxes with the confession that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God.
Matthew climaxes with Jesus as a ruling king who sends people out to make disciples.

The climax is the ascension. Jesus is Messiah and Lord. Those are his titles, right now.

And a king who is ruling has demands.

I have shifted away from believing that the most important thing is having the right belief system
about how the cross worked. It matters less to me now that we believe exactly the right things about
his death. Romans 1 says the purpose of heralding the gospel is to bring about the obedience of
faith.

Look at the parables. A king returns and judges his servants on their loyalty, on whether they did
his will while he was gone. Look at the sheep and the goats. Feeding a child, visiting someone sick.
Those acts are what distinguish them from the people who say "Lord, Lord" and were never known.

Maybe allegiant obedience is what God was after all along.

That is what the obedience of faith has come to mean to me. Allegiance is a more holistic idea of
faith. It takes in mind, body, and spirit. All of me, loyal.

### Faith and Works

I know this raises the tension between faith and works, at least the way I was brought up to
understand it. I am not the first person to notice it.

The whole book of James is about faith demonstrated through what you do. And there are passages I
used to dismiss because they did not fit how I saw scripture. Romans 2 is one of them, where what we
do seems to determine our judgment.

I am not done thinking about this. But this is where I think I am going.

### Where I Am Today

So what is the life that is asked of us? Everything is simplified.

Jesus began a new covenant, and his one command is love for God and love for one another. Everything
else can be encapsulated there.

This is not a rule based system where you do good things to earn your standing with God. That was
never possible. The death of Christ starts a new covenant, and we are given the Spirit, who instead
of handing us a list writes the law on our hearts.

The Spirit enables me to make choices that matter. Choices that show allegiance to Christ and to the
ethics of his kingdom, by the grace of a free gift.

It has brought a little more fire to my day to day. The opportunities I have throughout an ordinary
day are opportunities to show loyalty to a king who has ascended and who is ruling right now.

That is where I am landing. That is where I am today.

I am going to keep exploring, because I am not yet done.

### I Sing Again

All of this took about seven years.

I should say that during those years I also stepped away from church entirely. I searched around a
lot. That is a separate story.

Eventually I found a new church.

And I started singing again. I started finding the joy of singing again.
